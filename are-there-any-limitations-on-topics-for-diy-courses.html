Are There Any Limitations on Topics for DIY Courses for Individual Certificates?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

No. You can earn a Certificate of Completion on any topic — there is no list of approved subjects, and no human approves your topic. You earn the certificate by proving understanding through graded coursework tailored to the topic — a written case analysis for a knowledge topic, the work itself for a visual skill, or written questions and answers in the language for a language topic — graded by AI, 70 out of 100 to pass.

What the AI checks

The AI checks only that your coursework shows real understanding of the topic you chose — not whether the topic is academic, practical, or unusual. Learn it anywhere, prove it on NewU.

Where limits do appear

The limits only appear if you later want the certificate to count toward a major. Then the topic has to fit one of the major’s course types and meet the post-secondary standard — and a topic that is related but too narrow can be enhanced into a fuller course. See Do DIY courses have to meet a standard to count toward a major?, What happens if my DIY course topic is too narrow?, and Can I use a single-course certificate toward a major?.

For example, “How to Read a Bank Statement” earns a Certificate of Completion on its own, exactly as it stands. To count toward an Accounting major, it was enhanced into a fuller course on financial documents — and the original certificate stayed valid either way.

If you build the course inside a major

If you build the course inside a major instead, relevance is checked when you propose the topic — a topic that does not fit the major will not be accepted there. But you can still take the same topic as a stand-alone certificate.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a certificate for a hobby?
Yes. Any topic you can learn and prove understanding of can earn a Certificate of Completion.

Does the topic have to be academic?
No. Academic fit only matters if the certificate is going to count toward a major.

Can a topic be too narrow for a certificate?
No. Too narrow only matters for major credit — and then enhancement is the answer, not a refusal.

The bottom line

For a certificate on its own, any topic. For major credit, the topic has to fit — and that is a question for rollover, not for the certificate.
