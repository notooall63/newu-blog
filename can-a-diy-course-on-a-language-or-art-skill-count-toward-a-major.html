Can a DIY Course on a Language or an Art Skill Count Toward a Major?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

It can, but the bar is higher than for the certificate alone. A stand-alone certificate proves you learned the topic — a tailored assignment, graded by AI, 70 out of 100 to pass. For major credit, the post-secondary standard applies at rollover: the topic must fit one of the major’s course types and meet the substance requirements.

Practising a skill does not count as substance on its own

Practising a language, or making art, is real learning — and it earns the certificate. But the substance check for major credit looks at depth, citations, and long-form sources, and practice alone does not meet it. That is the difference between a certificate for the topic and a course that counts toward a major.

If the topic is related but too narrow: enhancement

If the topic fits the major but is too narrow for major credit, the AI may propose an enhanced course — a fuller post-secondary course on the topic. If you accept it and pass the enhanced coursework, it counts as one of the major’s 40 courses, and it takes $12.49 off the $999 one-time price. A full rollover takes $25 off. Your original certificate stays valid either way.

How to find out

The binding answer comes only from the rollover assessment, which runs when you ask for it. Before that, an admin can give you a general yes or no by email — including whether the major is even a possible fit for the topic. Frequently asked questions

Does my language certificate count toward a major?
Only if the major has a course type the topic fits, and the certificate passes the substance check at rollover.

If it does not count, do I lose the certificate?
No. It stays valid on its own, exactly as it stands.

Can I check before I start the coursework?
Ask an admin for a general answer by email, or run the rollover assessment for the binding verdict.

The bottom line

Yes, it can — at rollover, if it fits and meets the standard. The certificate is yours either way.
