Can I Get College Credit for Something I Taught Myself?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Not as transfer credit at an accredited college. NewU Institute is not an accredited university, and its certificates are not transfer credit. But that is not the end of the story: you can earn a verifiable Certificate of Completion that proves what you taught yourself, and that certificate can count toward a NewU major. For an employer — the person most likely to care — a verified record of real work is what matters.

The honest answer on transfer credit

Accredited colleges grant transfer credit for courses taken at other accredited institutions. Self-taught learning does not fit that system, because there is no accredited institution behind it to vouch for it. That is true whether you learned from a book, a video, or a free online course. NewU does not change that, and it does not pretend to: NewU Institute is not a college, university, or accredited institution, and its Certificate of Post-Secondary Education is not a degree and carries no accreditation. Anyone who tells you self-study automatically becomes transfer credit is selling you something.

What you can get instead

What NewU gives you is a verified record. You take a DIY Course on the topic you taught yourself — learn anywhere, then prove it through coursework and AI grading. You need 70 out of 100 to pass, the same bar as every other NewU course. When you pass, you earn a Certificate of Completion ($19.99), anchored to Bitcoin, that any employer can verify in seconds through a link you issue: the credential, your name, your photograph, and a VALID verdict. That certificate stays valid on its own, and it can also roll into a NewU major — one certificate counts as one course, and takes $25 off the $999 one-time fee ($12.49 if it needed enhancement), off the one-time fee only.

How it works on NewU

You propose the topic, submit a written case analysis with citations to the sources you used, and the AI grades it. You have up to 3 submissions per assignment, and your highest grade stands. If your topic is real but too narrow to count toward a major on its own, it is not rejected — the AI builds it into a full post-secondary course, and you do the coursework on the enhanced topic. There is no cap: all 40 courses in a major can be DIY Courses if you want them to be.

Frequently asked questions

The bottom line

You cannot turn self-taught learning into transfer credit at an accredited college. But you can turn it into a verified certificate that proves what you learned — and that certificate can count toward a NewU major. For most people, that proof is the point.
