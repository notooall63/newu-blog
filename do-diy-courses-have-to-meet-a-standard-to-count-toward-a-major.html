Do DIY Courses Have to Meet a Standard to Count Toward a Major?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Yes. A DIY Course counts toward a major only when it meets NewU’s post-secondary standard. You can learn anywhere — a book, a video, a free online course, your own study. But to count toward a major, the course has to be post-secondary level: real substance, real analysis, real evidence.

What the standard means

It means the course has to be a real course, not a sliver of one. The topic has to fit the part of the major it would count toward. The coursework has to show real analysis, not just a description of how to do something. It has to draw on real sources, cited properly, including at least one substantial source — not just a single short video. And it has to cover real breadth: a genuine subject, not one narrow trick.

NewU’s AI checks all of this itself, from the topic and the coursework you submit and the grade. There is no human approving topics in advance, and no separate hours rule. The check is on the work itself: does it show that you know the subject at a post-secondary level?

Why the standard exists

Because a Certificate of Post-Secondary Education has to mean the same thing no matter how a course was learned. A learner who took a listed course and a learner who designed their own both did post-secondary-level work in every course, whether listed or DIY. Without a standard, a narrow topic could count the same as a full course, and the certificate would stop meaning anything — to the learner, and to any employer checking it.

What it is not

The standard is not a punishment for learning efficiently, and it is not a second exam. It does not ask where you learned, or how long you spent. It asks only whether the coursework proves you know the subject. A strong, well-evidenced piece of work on a genuine topic passes. A thin topic does not get rejected outright, either — NewU’s AI helps build it into a full post-secondary course instead (see What happens if my DIY course topic is too narrow?).

Frequently asked questions

Does the standard apply to a stand-alone certificate, too?
A Certificate of Completion for a single course is earned by passing that course at 70 out of 100. The post-secondary standard is what decides whether that course can also count toward a major.

Who decides whether my course meets the standard?
NewU’s AI, from your coursework and grade — no human approval of topics, and no separate hours requirement.
