Do DIY Courses Include Art-Based Courses?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Yes. Visual and physical skills — drawing, painting, photography, crafts — are covered by stand-alone DIY courses. You submit the work itself, not an essay about it.

How it works

You propose the topic — for example, “How to Draw a Portrait” — and NewU’s AI creates an assignment tailored to it. For a visual skill, that means you upload an image of your work, plus a short written reflection on your process, your materials, and your techniques. The AI creates criteria tailored to the skill and grades the visible work itself, not just knowledge about the skill. The coursework is graded by AI, and you need 70 out of 100 to pass. The certificate names the topic you proposed.

No essay required

You do not have to write a long analysis about drawing to prove you can draw. The drawing is the proof. See Do I Have to Write an Essay for a DIY Course?

What is not covered yet

Spoken or musical performance, video-dependent movement, interactive software, and work that needs more than one artifact do not have a dedicated assignment shape yet. Those are separate decisions, not part of this one.

What about a major?

An art certificate can count toward a major, but only at rollover, and only if the topic fits one of the major’s course types and meets the post-secondary standard. Making art does not count as substance on its own. See Can a DIY Course on a Language or an Art Skill Count Toward a Major?

Frequently asked questions

What do I submit?
An image of your work, plus a short written reflection on how you made it.

Do I have to write an essay?
No. For a visual skill, the work itself is the proof.

Can I submit a photo of a painting?
Yes. A photo of the work is the work.

Does the AI judge the art?
It grades the visible work against criteria tailored to the skill — not just what you know about the skill, but what you made.

Does it cost extra?
No. An art certificate costs the same $19.99 as any stand-alone Certificate of Completion.

Where do I start?

The bottom line

Yes — art is in. The work is the proof.
