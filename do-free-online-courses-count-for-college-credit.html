Do Free Online Courses Count for College Credit?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Usually no. A free online course on its own does not carry college credit, because credit comes from an accredited institution that graded your work and keeps the record. What a free course gives you is the learning. To turn that learning into something that counts, you need someone to grade it and record it — and that is what NewU Institute does.

Why free courses do not carry credit by themselves

College credit is not just about what you learned. It is about who checked it. An accredited college awards credit because it set the curriculum, graded the work, and stands behind the record. A free online course has the teaching but none of that machinery: nobody grades you, nobody keeps a transcript, and no employer can verify that you finished it. The learning is real, but the credit is not — because there is no institution vouching for it.

What turns learning into credit

Grading and a record. On NewU Institute, the same free course content becomes the basis for graded coursework: you submit a written case analysis with citations, NewU’s AI grades it, and you need 70 out of 100 to pass. When you pass, the result is anchored to Bitcoin, and you earn a certificate — a Certificate of Completion for a single course ($19.99), or a Certificate of Post-Secondary Education for a full major of 40 courses. That is a verified record an employer can check in seconds. It is not transfer credit at an accredited college — NewU Institute is not accredited, and its certificates carry no accreditation — but it is proof of completed, graded work.
