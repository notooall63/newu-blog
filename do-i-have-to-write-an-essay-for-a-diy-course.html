Do I Have to Write an Essay for a DIY Course?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Only if the topic calls for it. The assignment is tailored to the topic — NewU’s AI creates the assignment and the rubric for that particular topic, and holds you to that rubric. The proof matches the skill.

For a knowledge topic: yes, a written analysis

For a topic you learn about — for example, “How to Read a Bank Statement” — the coursework is a written case analysis, 1,000–1,500 words, with citations where the assignment asks for them. For a knowledge topic, the written analysis is the proof: it shows you understand the subject and can apply it.

For a visual skill: no essay — the work itself

For a visual or physical skill — drawing, painting, photography, crafts — you submit the work itself as an image, plus a short written reflection on your process, materials, and techniques. The AI grades the visible work against criteria tailored to the skill. See Do DIY Courses Include Art-Based Courses?

For a language: no essay — written questions and answers in the language

For a language, the assignment is written questions and answers in the language, at the level you claimed. There is no speaking test and no audio. See Do DIY Courses Include Languages?

Inside a major, it is different

This tailored assignment is for a stand-alone certificate. A DIY course built inside a major still uses the post-secondary standard — a written case analysis — because it has to count as one of the major’s 40 courses. The post-secondary standard also applies at rollover, when a stand-alone certificate is used to count toward a major.

The pass mark is the same either way

Whatever the shape, the coursework is graded by AI and you need 70 out of 100 to pass.

Frequently asked questions

What if my topic is a mix of knowledge and skill?
The AI picks the shape that fits the topic.

Can I choose the shape myself?
No. The AI creates the assignment from the topic you proposed.

What if I like writing essays?
A knowledge topic will still ask for one — the written analysis is the right proof for what you learned.
