How Do I Learn Something on My Own and Prove It to Others That I Have Learned It?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Learn it anywhere, then prove it on NewU Institute. You study the subject your way — a book, a video, a free online course, your own study — and then you submit coursework on what you learned. NewU’s AI grades it, and if you pass you earn a Certificate of Completion: a permanent, blockchain-anchored record that any employer can verify in seconds. That is how self-taught learning becomes proof instead of a claim.

Step 1: Learn it your way

Pick a real source and work through it properly. A university’s free online course, a serious textbook, a documentary series — whatever teaches the subject well. NewU never makes you log into another platform or buy course materials. The learning is yours to do, in your own time, at your own pace. There is no deadline and no requirement to have learned it recently: you prove what you know now.

Step 2: Prove it with coursework

On NewU, proving it means submitting a written case analysis on the topic, with citations to the sources you used. The AI grades it, and you need 70 out of 100 to pass — the same bar as every other NewU course. You have up to 3 submissions for each assignment, and your highest grade stands, so a weak first attempt does not sink you. Random finished courses also get a spot check asking you to explain a piece of your own work in your own words, and a human reviewer is the backstop. That is what makes the proof trustworthy: the work is graded, and the grading is checked.

Step 3: Share the proof

When you pass, the result is anchored to Bitcoin and you receive a Certificate of Completion ($19.99 for a single course). You issue a verification grant to an employer’s email address — a link, not a login. Anyone with the link can open it and see the credential, your name, your photograph, and a VALID verdict, backed by the Bitcoin anchor. The link stays valid until you revoke it, and the employer needs no account. Your photograph is never printed on the certificate itself — it only appears on the verification page, so the employer can confirm the work is yours.
