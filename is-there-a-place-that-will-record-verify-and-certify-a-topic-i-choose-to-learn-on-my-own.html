Is There a Place That Will Record, Verify, and Certify a Topic I Choose to Learn on My Own?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Yes. NewU Institute will record, verify, and certify a topic you choose to learn on your own. You pick the topic, learn it anywhere, and prove it on NewU through coursework and AI grading. What you earn is a Certificate of Completion — a permanent, verifiable record that any employer can check for free.

How the record is made

It starts with a DIY Course. You propose the topic — something you learned from a book, a video, a free online course, or your own study — and then you submit coursework on it: a written case analysis, with citations to the sources you used. NewU’s AI grades it, and you need 70 out of 100 to pass. You have up to 3 submissions for each assignment, and your highest grade stands.

When you pass, the result is recorded and anchored to Bitcoin, the public blockchain. That anchor is permanent: it cannot be edited, deleted, or lost, and it does not depend on NewU staying in business. Your certificate carries a QR code that links straight to the verification page.

How an employer checks it

You issue a verification grant to the employer’s email address — a link, not a login. Anyone with the link can open it and see the credential, your name, your photograph, and a VALID verdict, backed by the Bitcoin anchor. The link stays valid until you revoke it. The employer needs no account, no phone call, and no transcript request. And your photograph is never printed on the certificate itself — it only appears on the verification page, so the employer can confirm the work is yours.

What the certificate is — and is not

A Certificate of Completion is proof that you learned a specific topic and passed NewU’s graded coursework on it. It is not a degree, and NewU Institute is not an accredited university — the certificate carries no accreditation. What it is, is a verified record of real work: what you studied, when, and how it was graded, checked by mathematics instead of by trust. And it can roll into a major later — one certificate counts as one course, and takes $25 off the $999 one-time fee ($12.49 if it needed enhancement), off the one-time fee only.
