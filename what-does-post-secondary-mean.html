What Does “Post-Secondary” Mean?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Post-secondary means education after high school, at college level. It is the level NewU teaches at — not high school, not a hobby course, but college-level work.

What the word actually means

“Secondary” is high school. “Post-secondary” is everything after it: college, university, and any education at college level. When NewU calls itself a post-secondary education platform, it is saying the work is college-level — the subjects, the depth, and the standard are the same as what you would meet in a college major. A NewU major is 40 courses across Major Core, General Education, and Free Electives, the same shape as a college degree program. Coursework is a written case analysis graded by AI at 70 out of 100 to pass.

What it does not mean

Post-secondary does not mean accredited, and it does not mean a degree. NewU Institute is not a college, university, or accredited institution, and its Certificate of Post-Secondary Education is not a degree and carries no accreditation. The word describes the level of the education, not who recognizes it. What NewU offers instead of accreditation is verification: every completed course is anchored to Bitcoin, so any employer can independently verify what you studied, when, and how it was graded.

Frequently asked questions

Is post-secondary the same as college?
It is the same level. College is one kind of post-secondary education; NewU is another, at the same level but without accreditation or a degree.

Does a post-secondary certificate count as a degree?
No. A Certificate of Post-Secondary Education is proof of completed, graded work — not a degree, and not transfer credit at an accredited college.
