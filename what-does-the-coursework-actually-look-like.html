What Does the Coursework Actually Look Like?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

A 1,000–1,500-word case analysis with citations, graded by AI at 70 out of 100 to pass. Not a multiple-choice quiz — a piece of written work that shows you understand the subject and can apply it.

What you actually write

For each course, NewU’s AI generates an assignment built from the course content — usually a case analysis set in a real-world situation, with a rubric that spells out what good work looks like. You write your analysis in your own words, citing the sources you used, and submit it on the platform. There is no exam hall, no timer, and no due date: it is self-paced, and you submit when you are ready.

How it is graded

Your coursework is graded by AI against the rubric, and you need 70 out of 100 to pass — the same bar for every course, listed or DIY. The grading is re-checked for consistency, and anything flagged gets a human review. You have up to 3 submissions per coursework, and your highest grade stands, so a weak first attempt does not sink you. Random finished courses also get a spot check asking you to explain a piece of your own work in your own words, and a human reviewer is the backstop.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to write in a particular style?
No. You write a case analysis in your own words, with citations to the sources you used. The rubric tells you what the grader is looking for.

What if I fail?
You get up to 3 submissions per coursework, your highest grade stands, and you can retake a course as many times as you like. Your plan covers the grading — there is no extra charge.

Where do I start?
At newu.institute — explore the 100 majors, or design your own. Questions? Email newuinstitute@aol.com.

The bottom line

Coursework on NewU is a written case analysis, not a quiz: 1,000 to 1,500 words, with citations, graded by AI at 70 to pass, with retakes if you need them.
