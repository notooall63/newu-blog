What Is a Spot Check?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

A spot check picks 1 in 4 finished courses at random and asks you to explain a piece of your own work in your own words. It is how NewU keeps grading honest — and no action is ever taken without a human-reviewed decision.

How a spot check works

After you finish a course, there is a one-in-four chance it is randomly chosen for a spot check. You are asked to explain a piece of your own coursework in your own words — not to redo the assignment, just to show you understand the work you submitted. If your explanation is solid, that is the end of it. If the explanation comes up short, a human reviewer looks at it. Nothing is decided by the AI alone: no action is ever taken without a human-reviewed decision.

Why spot checks exist

Coursework on NewU is graded by AI, and a certificate that an employer can verify is only worth something if the grade behind it was earned. Spot checks are the check on the learner — the counterpart to the consistency checks on the grader. They are random, not targeted: being picked does not mean you are suspected of anything. Most learners will be spot-checked at some point across a 40-course major, and most will pass it without a second thought, because they did the work.

Frequently asked questions

Can I fail a spot check?
A spot check is not a grade. If your explanation comes up short, a human reviewer looks at it — and no action is ever taken without a human-reviewed decision.

Does a spot check delay my certificate?
No. A spot check does not hold up your course credit while it is reviewed.

The bottom line

A spot check is a random 1-in-4 ask to explain your own work in your own words. It keeps the certificate honest — and a human always has the last word.
