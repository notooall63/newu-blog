What Is the Best Way to Learn a College Subject on My Own?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Pick a real college-level source, study it properly, and then prove it. The best way to learn a college subject on your own is to use the same material a college course uses — a university’s free online course, a serious textbook — study it actively instead of skimming it, and then submit graded coursework on it. On NewU Institute, that last step turns self-study into a verifiable Certificate of Completion that any employer can check.

Pick the right material

Start with something a college actually teaches. Free online courses from real universities are the best place to begin: the same lectures, readings, and problem sets as the paid course, at no cost. A serious textbook works too. What matters is that the material is college-level — not a summary, not a five-minute video, but the real subject taught properly. If you would not find it on a college syllabus, it is probably not the right source.

Study like it counts

Watching a lecture is not learning. Work through the material: take notes, do the exercises, test yourself, and write about what you learned. The single best habit is to explain the subject in your own words — if you cannot explain it, you do not know it yet. That is also, not coincidentally, what NewU’s spot check asks you to do: explain a piece of your own work in your own words. Study for understanding, not for completion, and set your own pace. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a textbook?
Not necessarily. A university’s free online course, a book, a video, or your own study can all work — what matters is that the source is college-level and you study it properly.

How do I know I am learning the right things?
Follow a real college syllabus for the subject, and check your understanding by writing about it. The coursework itself is the test: if you can write a case analysis with citations and pass at 70 out of 100, you have learned it.
