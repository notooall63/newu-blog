Who Grades My Coursework — a Person or an AI?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

An AI grades your coursework. The grading is re-checked for consistency, and a human reviewer is the backstop for anything flagged. A person never grades your work first — but a person always has the last word when something is questioned.

How AI grading works on NewU

Your coursework — a 1,000–1,500-word case analysis with citations — is graded by AI against a rubric that spells out what good work looks like. You need 70 out of 100 to pass, the same bar for every course. The grading does not happen once and stand alone: it is re-checked for consistency, so a single odd grade cannot decide your result. Random finished courses also get a spot check asking you to explain a piece of your own work in your own words. If anything is flagged — a spot check that comes up short, or a question about authorship — a human reviewer looks at it, and no action is ever taken without a human-reviewed decision.

Why AI, and why it is checked

AI grading is what makes NewU possible at $79.99 a month or $999 once: a human grading every assignment would cost what a college charges. But NewU does not trust the AI blindly. The consistency checks, the spot checks, and the human reviewer exist because grading has to be fair — and because a certificate that an employer can verify is only worth something if the grade behind it was earned. Your work is never used to train AI models.

Frequently asked questions

Can I appeal a grade?
You have up to 3 submissions per coursework, and your highest grade stands. If you believe a grade is wrong, the review process — consistency checks and human review of anything flagged — is how it is caught.

Does a person ever read my work?
Yes, when it matters: a human reviewer reads your work when a spot check or a flag calls for it. Day-to-day grading is done by AI.

The bottom line

AI grades your coursework, consistency checks keep it honest, and a human reviewer is the backstop. The grade is earned — and that is what makes the certificate worth verifying.
