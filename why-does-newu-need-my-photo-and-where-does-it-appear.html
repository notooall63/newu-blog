Why Does NewU Need My Photo, and Where Does It Appear?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

Your photo’s only job is verifying the work is yours. It is NOT on your certificate. It IS shown to an employer on the verification page, next to your name and the VALID verdict.

Why a photo at all

NewU does not check who you are with a government ID — it never verifies your name, date of birth, or address against any ID or database. The only two things that must be real are your email address and your photograph. Your photograph is your verification photo: it is how future verifiers confirm the certificate belongs to you. When an employer opens the verification link you issued, they see your name, your photograph, and the VALID verdict, and they can match the face to the person in front of them. That is the whole point of it.

Where it appears — and where it does not

Your photograph appears in two places: on your account page, where it is shown back to you, and on the employer verification page, next to your name and the VALID verdict. It does NOT appear on your Certificate of Post-Secondary Education or your Certificate of Completion — the certificate carries no photograph. It is never used for anything else, and NewU does not run face matching or any biometric identification on it. Once you confirm your photograph, it is locked; one optional update becomes available every 15 years. If you delete your whole account, your photograph is deleted with it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a photo of something else?
No. It must be a photograph of yourself — it is how a verifier confirms the work is yours.

Who else can see my photo?
Only you, and anyone you issue a verification link to. NewU reviewers and administrators may view it when carrying out grading checks, spot checks, or fraud review. It is never shared with advertisers or data brokers, and your work is never used to train AI models.

The bottom line

Your photo proves the work is yours. It shows up for the employer checking your certificate — and nowhere else that matters.
