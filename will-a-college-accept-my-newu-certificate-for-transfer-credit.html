Will a College Accept My NewU Certificate for Transfer Credit?

By NewU Institute

The short answer

No — not as transfer credit at an accredited college. NewU Institute is not an accredited university, and its certificates are not transfer credit. The certificate is proof of completed, graded work for employers and for your own record.

Why colleges will not take it as transfer credit

Accredited colleges grant transfer credit for courses taken at other accredited institutions. NewU is not an accredited institution, and its Certificate of Post-Secondary Education is not a degree and carries no accreditation. That is the honest limit, and NewU says so plainly: it does not claim accreditation by any government agency or educational authority, and it does not pretend its certificate is a degree. Anyone who tells you a NewU certificate automatically becomes college credit is selling you something.

What the certificate is good for

What the certificate proves is real: that you completed 40 courses in a major (or one DIY Course, for a Certificate of Completion), that your coursework was graded by AI at 70 out of 100 to pass, and that every completed course is anchored to Bitcoin so any employer can independently verify what you studied, when, and how it was graded. For an employer — the person most likely to care — a verified record of real work is what matters. It is also a record for yourself: your transcript is a verified, printable record of your courses and progress, free to view and download forever.

Frequently asked questions

Could a college ever accept it?
That is up to each college, and NewU makes no promise. The certificate is not transfer credit, and no college is obliged to treat it as such.

Does it count toward a NewU major?
Yes. A Certificate of Completion can roll into a relative NewU major — one certificate counts as one course, and takes $25 off the $999 one-time fee ($12.49 if it was enhanced), off the one-time fee only.

The bottom line

No college has to accept a NewU certificate as transfer credit — and NewU will not tell you otherwise. What it is, is proof of completed work that any employer can verify in seconds.
